## Across all steps

**Accepted method**

- Report what was done at every stage, with a flow diagram of records identified, screened, excluded (with reasons) and included. *PRISMA 2020: Page MJ et al. BMJ 2021;372:n71* (https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.n71)
- The authors stay accountable for AI use; justify and validate a tool for the specific review, keep human oversight, and report any AI that makes or suggests judgements. *AI in evidence synthesis: Position statement on AI use in evidence synthesis across Cochrane, the Campbell Collaboration, JBI and the Collaboration for Environmental Evidence (endorsing RAISE), 2025* (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12594113/)
- Do not rely on AI to fully automate any step; use it for quality assurance (e.g. flagging missed studies or extraction errors); a human verifies all AI outputs and makes the final decisions; state the tools, their tasks, model versions and prompts. *AI in rapid reviews: Gartlehner G et al. Responsible integration of artificial intelligence in rapid reviews: a position statement from the Cochrane Rapid Reviews Methods Group. Cochrane Evid Synth Methods 2025;4:e70063* (https://doi.org/10.1002/cesm.70063)

**In Sieve**

- ✓ Every AI step is a fixed prompt + JSON schema, stored per run with model and date, next to the human decision; prompts can be downloaded.
- ✓ Evidence quotes are checked word for word against the paper and can be opened highlighted in the text.

## 1 · Protocol

**Accepted method**

- Define the question and eligibility criteria before searching, and register or publish the protocol (e.g. PROSPERO, OSF). *PRISMA-P: Moher D et al. Syst Rev 2015;4:1* (https://doi.org/10.1186/2046-4053-4-1)
- Rapid reviews: involve knowledge users in the question (rec. 1); a protocol before starting, registered where possible (rec. 2); clear eligibility criteria and any limits (rec. 3). *Cochrane rapid reviews: Garritty C et al. Updated recommendations for the Cochrane rapid review methods guidance for rapid reviews of effectiveness. BMJ 2024;384:e076335* (https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-076335)
- Scoping reviews: an a priori protocol with population, concept and context; report with PRISMA-ScR. *JBI scoping reviews: Peters MDJ et al. Updated methodological guidance for the conduct of scoping reviews. JBI Evid Synth 2020;18(10):2119-26* (https://doi.org/10.11124/JBIES-20-00167)

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Protocol | Registered protocol before searching (PRISMA-P; e.g. PROSPERO). | Protocol before starting, registered where possible (rec. 2). | A priori protocol with PCC (JBI). | AI drafts criteria, search and fields; your approval fixes them, dated, as the scan's protocol. |

**In Sieve**

- ✓ Question, PICO / PICo / SPIDER / PCC, eligibility criteria with the consequence of failing each one; purpose-first choice of review type.
- ✓ Start from an existing document (ticket, proposal, criteria flowchart).
- ✗ Export in PRISMA-P / registration format and a frozen, dated protocol version for the full review.

## 2 · Search

**Accepted method**

- Search CENTRAL, MEDLINE and Embase if available (C24, mandatory); trial registers where relevant (C27, mandatory); reference lists (C30, mandatory); specialist databases (C25) and grey literature (C28) highly desirable; document the search (C36). *MECIR: Methodological Expectations of Cochrane Intervention Reviews, version August 2023* (https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/mecir-manual)
- All search strategies should be peer reviewed before being run, by a librarian or information specialist (4.2.2). *Cochrane Handbook ch. 4: Searching for and selecting studies, version 6.5.1, 2025* (https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-04)
- Peer review the strategy on six elements: translation of the question, Boolean/proximity operators, subject headings, text words, spelling/syntax/line numbers, limits and filters. *PRESS 2015: McGowan J et al. J Clin Epidemiol 2016;75:40-6* (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2016.01.021)
- Rapid reviews: involve an information specialist (rec. 4); at least two bibliographic databases, e.g. two of MEDLINE, CENTRAL and Embase (rec. 5); PRESS for the primary strategy, at minimum a check for errors (rec. 6); justify grey literature (rec. 7). *Cochrane rapid reviews: Garritty C et al. Updated recommendations for the Cochrane rapid review methods guidance for rapid reviews of effectiveness. BMJ 2024;384:e076335* (https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-076335)
- Test the strategy against known relevant studies (relative recall). *Relative recall: Sampson M et al. BMC Med Res Methodol 2006;6:33* (https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2288-6-33)
- Embase, MEDLINE, Web of Science and Google Scholar together gave the best recall in their study. *Database combinations: Bramer WM et al. Syst Rev 2017;6:245* (https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-017-0644-y)
- Report every database with its platform, the full strategy, limits, search date and number of records. *PRISMA-S: Rethlefsen ML et al. Syst Rev 2021;10:39* (https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-020-01542-z)

| | Systematic review | Rapid review | Scoping review | Quick scan (Sieve) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Databases | CENTRAL, MEDLINE, Embase (MECIR C24) + registers, specialist databases, grey literature. | At least two bibliographic databases (rec. 5). | Broad enough for the scope (JBI). | PubMed + Europe PMC preprints/PMC, named as a limitation. |
| Peer review | Strongly recommended before running (Handbook 4.2.2; PRESS). | PRESS for the primary strategy (rec. 6). | Recommended. | PRESS checklist on the search stage; peer reviewer's name recorded. |

**In Sieve**

- ✓ Validation and hold-out sets, tested live in PubMed (recall, missed articles and why), and recall per database after importing the exports.
- ✓ Translations of a MEDLINE (Ovid) strategy to Embase, Web of Science, PsycInfo, ACM and the Cochrane Library (CENTRAL), with a check list and an assistant review; counts per line.
- ◐ PRESS: a six-element checklist with the peer reviewer's name in the quick scan; no PRESS form in step 2 yet.
- ◐ PRISMA-S: strategies and counts per line are kept; platform, search date and limits per database are not recorded as fields yet.
- ✓ Cochrane Library (CENTRAL) translation of line strategies and of the concept blocks (search appendix).
- ✓ ClinicalTrials.gov searched live from the quick scan (registrations counted separately in the PRISMA flow); query translation for the WHO ICTRP portal, which has no free API (manual export).
- ✗ Google Scholar, CINAHL and Scopus translations; an importer for ICTRP XML exports.

## 3 · Records and deduplication

**Accepted method**

- Remove duplicates in fixed passes from strict to loose field combinations, with normalised page numbers; check the loose matches by hand. *Bramer deduplication: Bramer WM et al. J Med Libr Assoc 2016;104(3):240-3; correction 2017;105(1):111* (https://doi.org/10.3163/1536-5050.104.3.014)
- Document the selection process in enough detail for a flow diagram (C41). *MECIR: Methodological Expectations of Cochrane Intervention Reviews, version August 2023* (https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/mecir-manual)
- Report records identified per source and the number of duplicates removed. *PRISMA 2020: Page MJ et al. BMJ 2021;372:n71* (https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.n71)

- ✓ Bramer passes A-G plus DOI/PMID, run after every import; strict matches linked automatically, the rest in a review list (docs/deduplication.md).
- ✓ Duplicates are linked, not deleted; overlap per database (already found / only here).

## 5 · Full text

**Accepted method**

- Retrieve the full text of every record that passes title/abstract screening; report reports sought and reports not retrieved. *PRISMA 2020: Page MJ et al. BMJ 2021;372:n71* (https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.n71)

| | Systematic review | Rapid review | Scoping review | Quick scan (Sieve) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full texts | All potentially eligible reports, via the library and authors if needed. | Same; report what was not retrieved. | Same. | Open-access texts only; the others are judged on the abstract, reported as such. |

**In Sieve**

## 6 · Screening

**Accepted method**

- Use (at least) two people working independently to decide whether each study meets the eligibility criteria, with a predefined way to resolve disagreements (C39, mandatory); dual title/abstract screening is desirable, not mandatory. *MECIR: Methodological Expectations of Cochrane Intervention Reviews, version August 2023* (https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/mecir-manual)
- Pilot the criteria on a sample of reports (e.g. six to eight); decide on full texts with two people independently (4.6.4). Automatic elimination of records by machine learning was not recommended at the time of writing (mid-2023); classifiers can reduce manual screening by 30% to over 90%, sometimes at a loss of up to 5% sensitivity (4.6.6.2). *Cochrane Handbook ch. 4: Searching for and selecting studies, version 6.5.1, 2025* (https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-04)
- Single-reviewer title/abstract screening misses studies (median 5%, 13% for less experienced reviewers). *Single vs. dual screening: Waffenschmidt S et al. BMC Med Res Methodol 2019;19:132* (https://doi.org/10.1186/s12874-019-0782-0)
- Rapid reviews: pilot screening (rec. 8); dual independent screening of a proportion of records (e.g. 20%), check agreement, continue with single screening if κ ≥ 0.8 (rec. 9); the same for full texts. *Cochrane rapid reviews: Garritty C et al. Updated recommendations for the Cochrane rapid review methods guidance for rapid reviews of effectiveness. BMJ 2024;384:e076335* (https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-076335)
- AI may re-examine records excluded by a single reviewer; a human makes every final decision. *AI in rapid reviews: Gartlehner G et al. Responsible integration of artificial intelligence in rapid reviews: a position statement from the Cochrane Rapid Reviews Methods Group. Cochrane Evid Synth Methods 2025;4:e70063* (https://doi.org/10.1002/cesm.70063)
- Ways to integrate AI in screening, from most to least robust; among them dual-independent screening with an AI reviewer, where another reviewer resolves records the AI excluded and the human included. Full automation (AI alone deciding on inclusion) performs poorly and should be avoided. *AI for title/abstract screening: Hamel C et al. Guidance for using artificial intelligence for title and abstract screening while conducting knowledge syntheses. BMC Med Res Methodol 2021;21:285* (https://doi.org/10.1186/s12874-021-01451-2)
- GPT models as independent second screeners of titles and abstracts: a proof of concept against human double screening, with common guidelines. *GPT as second screener: Vembye MH et al. Generative pretrained transformer models can function as highly reliable second screeners of titles and abstracts in systematic reviews: a proof of concept and common guidelines. Psychol Methods 2025* (https://doi.org/10.1037/met0000769)
- Scoping reviews: source selection at title/abstract and full-text level by two or more reviewers independently, after pilot testing; the protocol states how disagreements are resolved (10.2.6). *JBI Manual, scoping reviews: Peters MDJ et al. Chapter 10: Scoping reviews. In: Aromataris E et al. (eds). JBI Manual for Evidence Synthesis* (https://jbi-global.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/MANUAL/pages/355862497/10.+Scoping+reviews)
- With prioritised (active learning) screening, use an explicit, conservative stopping rule. *SAFE stopping procedure: Boetje J, van de Schoot R. Syst Rev 2024;13:81* (https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-024-02502-7)

| | Systematic review | Rapid review | Scoping review | Quick scan (Sieve) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Who screens | Two people independently; titles/abstracts by one acceptable, full texts by two (MECIR C39; Handbook 4.6.4). | Pilot; two people on ~20%, single screening if κ ≥ 0.8 (rec. 8-9). | Two or more reviewers independently, titles/abstracts and full texts, after a pilot (JBI Manual 10.2.6). | You screen every record blind and the AI is the independent second reviewer; you resolve the disagreements (Hamel 2021). Or two people blind with the AI as a check; or, faster, blind calibration (κ) then the AI suggestion shown. |

**In Sieve**

- ✓ Model runs per criterion with verified quotes; sensitivity, specificity and κ against your decisions; disagreements first; full-text exclusions require a reason.
- ✓ Quick scan: you screen every record blind with the AI as independent second reviewer and resolve the disagreements (κ shown); or, faster, a blind calibration sample and then the AI suggestion shown.
- ✓ Two people: a blind copy of the project for the second reviewer (here or on another computer, without your decisions or model runs); their decisions come back as a small file; κ between you and a consensus step that keeps both original decisions.
- ✓ Quick scan, two-person mode: both screen blind, consensus on disagreements, then the AI flags records you both excluded that it would keep (Cochrane RR AI position: AI to flag missed studies).
- ✗ Prioritised screening with a stopping rule such as SAFE.

## 7 · Data extraction and risk of bias

**Accepted method**

- Use a piloted data collection form (C43, mandatory); two people independently extract outcome data (C46, mandatory) and preferably study characteristics (C45, highly desirable). *MECIR: Methodological Expectations of Cochrane Intervention Reviews, version August 2023* (https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/mecir-manual)
- More than one person should extract data from every report; at minimum, data needing interpretation and data critical to the results are extracted independently by two people (5.5.2). *Cochrane Handbook ch. 5: Collecting data, version 6.5, 2024* (https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-05)
- Single extraction produces more errors than double extraction. *Single vs. double extraction: Buscemi N et al. J Clin Epidemiol 2006;59:697-703* (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16765272/)
- Rapid reviews: limit extraction to the most important fields (rec. 10); pilot (rec. 11); one person extracts, a second verifies critical data (rec. 12). *Cochrane rapid reviews: Garritty C et al. Updated recommendations for the Cochrane rapid review methods guidance for rapid reviews of effectiveness. BMJ 2024;384:e076335* (https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-076335)
- Assess the risk of bias of each study result that contributes to an outcome in the summary of findings, with RoB 2 for randomized trials (C52, mandatory); (at least) two people independently, with a predefined way to resolve disagreements (C53, mandatory); justify each judgement (C54) and record its source, e.g. a quotation (C55). *MECIR: Methodological Expectations of Cochrane Intervention Reviews, version August 2023* (https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/mecir-manual)
- Rapid reviews: validated, study-design-specific tools (rec. 14); at least for the most important outcomes (rec. 15); one person assesses and a second verifies the judgements (rec. 16). *Cochrane rapid reviews: Garritty C et al. Updated recommendations for the Cochrane rapid review methods guidance for rapid reviews of effectiveness. BMJ 2024;384:e076335* (https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-076335)
- Five domains, judged low risk, some concerns or high risk; the overall generally follows the worst domain, and several domains with some concerns can make it high. *RoB 2: Sterne JAC et al. BMJ 2019;366:l4898* (https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l4898)
- Seven domains for non-randomised studies of interventions, judged low, moderate, serious or critical (or no information); the overall is at least as severe as the worst domain. *ROBINS-I: Sterne JAC et al. BMJ 2016;355:i4919* (https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.i4919)
- Scoping reviews: data extraction is called charting; pilot it with at least two members of the team (10.2.7). *JBI Manual, scoping reviews: Peters MDJ et al. Chapter 10: Scoping reviews. In: Aromataris E et al. (eds). JBI Manual for Evidence Synthesis* (https://jbi-global.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/MANUAL/pages/355862497/10.+Scoping+reviews)
- Use a tool that fits the study type: e.g. PROBAST+AI for (AI) prediction models, RoB 2, ROBINS-I, QUADAS-2. *PROBAST+AI: Moons KGM et al. BMJ 2025;388:e082505* (https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2024-082505)

| | Systematic review | Rapid review | Scoping review | Quick scan (Sieve) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Extraction | Two people independently for outcome data (MECIR C46); piloted form (C43). | One extracts, a second verifies critical data (rec. 12). | Data charting, piloted by at least two team members (JBI Manual 10.2.7). | AI extracts with quotes; you check every value against its quote. |
| Risk of bias | Validated tool (RoB 2 for RCTs), two people independently (C52-C53). | Validated, design-specific tool; one assesses, a second verifies (rec. 14-16). | Optional (PRISMA-ScR items 12 and 16). | Optional appraisal stage: the tool for the design, AI drafts with quotes, you judge every domain; or skipped and named as a limitation. |

**In Sieve**

- ✓ Every value with a verified quote; two runs compared field by field; the human verifies each cell (disagreements and unverified quotes first).
- ✓ Pilot run and run checks (completeness, verified quotes, core-only rule).
- ✓ Risk of bias / quality: RoB 2, ROBINS-I, QUADAS-2, PROBAST+AI and the JBI qualitative checklist; AI drafts per domain with a quote checked word for word, a person judges every domain, the overall follows the tool's rule where it has one; traffic-light table, summary per domain, CSV.
- ✓ Second reviewer for extraction and risk of bias in the blind copy: values compared field by field, judgements domain by domain, consensus recorded.
- ◐ QUADAS-2 has been superseded by QUADAS-3 (2026) and ROBINS-I version 2 is a draft; both are still to be added.

## 8 · Report

**Accepted method**

- Flow diagram and the 27-item checklist. *PRISMA 2020: Page MJ et al. BMJ 2021;372:n71* (https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.n71)
- Search appendix with every full strategy. *PRISMA-S: Rethlefsen ML et al. Syst Rev 2021;10:39* (https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-020-01542-z)
- Rapid reviews: describe the restricted methods and discuss their limitations (rec. 24); GRADE if time and resources allow, with a summary of findings table and footnotes (rec. 21), limited to the main comparison and critical outcomes (rec. 22), one person rates and a second verifies (rec. 23). *Cochrane rapid reviews: Garritty C et al. Updated recommendations for the Cochrane rapid review methods guidance for rapid reviews of effectiveness. BMJ 2024;384:e076335* (https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-076335)
- Assess the certainty of the body of evidence for each outcome with the five GRADE considerations (C74, mandatory) and justify every judgement (C75, mandatory). *MECIR: Methodological Expectations of Cochrane Intervention Reviews, version August 2023* (https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/mecir-manual)
- Randomised trials start at high certainty and non-randomised studies at low (or high with ROBINS-I, rating down for risk of bias); rate down for risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias; rate up for a large effect, a dose-response gradient or plausible confounding. *Cochrane Handbook ch. 14: Completing 'Summary of findings' tables and grading the certainty of the evidence, version 6.5, 2024* (https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-14)
- For qualitative findings: confidence from methodological limitations, coherence, adequacy of data and relevance, starting from high confidence; explain every assessment. *GRADE-CERQual: Lewin S et al. Implement Sci 2018;13(Suppl 1):2* (https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-017-0688-3)
- A transparent statement of AI use and its validation. *AI in evidence synthesis: Position statement on AI use in evidence synthesis across Cochrane, the Campbell Collaboration, JBI and the Collaboration for Environmental Evidence (endorsing RAISE), 2025* (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12594113/)

| | Systematic review | Rapid review | Scoping review | Quick scan (Sieve) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reporting | PRISMA 2020 + PRISMA-S; certainty of evidence (GRADE, MECIR C74-C75). | PRISMA; GRADE if time allows (rec. 21-23); the restricted methods and their limitations (rec. 24). | PRISMA-ScR. | Labelled an evidence scan; optional GRADE / CERQual per finding; limitations written from what was done; AI use disclosure. |

**In Sieve**

- ✓ PRISMA 2020 counts and diagram, inclusion/exclusion per criterion, a methods draft built from what was run, a search appendix with all strategies and translations.
- ✓ PRISMA 2020, PRISMA-ScR and PRISMA-S checklists, each item with what Sieve holds for this project and where (Markdown export with a page column).
- ✓ Certainty per outcome or finding: GRADE (with the ROBINS-I start and three levels down for risk of bias, GRADE 18) or GRADE-CERQual; AI drafts, the person rates with a reason for every concern; summary-of-findings table with footnotes.
- ✓ AI use statement for the full review, built from the stored runs.

## Quick scan

**Accepted method**

- No step fully automated; AI as a check next to one human reviewer; the human verifies all AI outputs and makes the final decisions; disclose tools, tasks, model versions and prompts. *AI in rapid reviews: Gartlehner G et al. Responsible integration of artificial intelligence in rapid reviews: a position statement from the Cochrane Rapid Reviews Methods Group. Cochrane Evid Synth Methods 2025;4:e70063* (https://doi.org/10.1002/cesm.70063)
- The scan borrows the rapid review safeguards: protocol first (rec. 2), PRESS (rec. 6), pilot and calibration with κ (rec. 8-9), verified extraction (rec. 12), explicit limitations (rec. 24). *Cochrane rapid reviews: Garritty C et al. Updated recommendations for the Cochrane rapid review methods guidance for rapid reviews of effectiveness. BMJ 2024;384:e076335* (https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-076335)
- Accountability stays with the author; AI use is reported. *AI in evidence synthesis: Position statement on AI use in evidence synthesis across Cochrane, the Campbell Collaboration, JBI and the Collaboration for Environmental Evidence (endorsing RAISE), 2025* (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12594113/)

| | Systematic review | Rapid review | Scoping review | Quick scan (Sieve) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | Not a systematic review. | Not a rapid review: fewer databases, no risk of bias, no GRADE (rec. 5, 14-23). | Can seed a scoping review's search and charting. | An AI-assisted evidence scan: a fast, transparent first look that continues as a full review in the same project. |

**In Sieve**

- ✓ Plan approved as a dated protocol; PRESS checklist; blind calibration with κ; every record and every value decided or checked by you; summary approval; limitations and AI use disclosure written from what was done.
- ✓ Optional appraisal stage (risk of bias / quality) and certainty per finding (GRADE / CERQual); a two-person screening mode and a blind copy for a second reviewer.
- ✓ Auto mode: every AI step runs unattended and writes a manuscript draft (methods and results from what was run; introduction and discussion by the model) labelled UNVERIFIED AI DRAFT, with per section what a person has checked; no human decision, check or rating is written by the run.
